What we treat

Four problems people put off talking about

Most patients arrive after months of waiting. Symptoms are easier to settle when they are seen early, so there is no reason to wait for the pain to get worse.

Piles (haemorrhoids)

Bleeding while passing stool, itching, swelling or a lump near the anus. Graded on examination, then treated according to how advanced it is.

Anal fissure

Sharp, burning pain during and after passing stool, often with a small streak of blood. Usually linked to hard stool and constipation.

Fistula-in-ano

A discharging opening near the anus that keeps coming back. Needs proper assessment — this is not a condition to self-treat at home.

Constipation & digestion

The cause behind most of the above. Diet, water, routine and simple Ayurvedic support, so the problem is less likely to return.

Your visit

What actually happens when you come in

No surprises, no long waits, no lectures. Here is the whole visit, start to finish.

Call and pick a time

Phone the clinic and take a slot that suits you. Tell us only what you are comfortable saying on the phone — the rest is for the consulting room.

Consultation and examination

The doctor listens to your history, examines you privately, and tells you exactly what the problem is and how advanced it is. Your first consultation is free.

A plan you understand

You are told the treatment options, roughly how long it takes, what it costs and what you need to change at home. You decide from there.

Follow-up until it settles

Review visits track how you are healing, and the diet advice continues after treatment ends — because relapse usually starts at the dining table.

Questions

Things people ask before booking

Will the first visit be embarrassing?

It is a condition the doctor sees every single day, and the consultation happens behind a closed door. You will be examined only as much as is needed to know what is going on.

Is Ayurvedic treatment an alternative to surgery?

For many early and moderate cases, yes — medicines, local treatment and diet correction can control symptoms well. Advanced cases, or complicated fistulas, may still need a surgical procedure. The honest answer for your case comes after the examination, not before it.

How long does treatment take?

It depends entirely on the condition and its stage. Fissures often settle in a few weeks; piles and fistula take longer and need follow-up. You will be given a realistic time frame at the first visit.

Do I need an appointment?

Calling ahead is better, so you are not waiting. Walk-ins are seen when there is a free slot.

Can women consult here?

Yes. Piles and fissures are very common during and after pregnancy. Do mention on the phone if you would prefer a particular time or an attendant present.

What should I bring?

Any earlier prescriptions, reports or scans relating to the problem, and a list of medicines you currently take.
